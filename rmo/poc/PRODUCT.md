# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

- **Bank business stakeholders** (customer side). They read the readiness report's summary to understand migration
  scope and risk and to sign off a wave. They need the headline split (what converts, what doesn't, why) without
  query-level detail.
- **Bank BusinessObjects administrators and report owners** (customer side). They read the detail sections to
  act: fix, rebuild, or retire reports that won't convert, and check what changes in the ones that will. They know
  universes, queries, folders and Report Scheduler jobs.
- **FIS migration team** (internal). Generates the reports from the POC UI (`wwwroot/index.html`) and sends them
  to customers.

## Product Purpose

`RmoReportingPoc` is the reporting layer for the Report Migration Orchestrator (RMO), which moves a bank's SAP
BusinessObjects Webi/Excel reports from Oracle-backed universes to Snowflake-backed universes. It turns a
discovery snapshot (`Data/*-discovery.json`) into customer-facing reports: which reports will convert, which will
not and why, and which scheduled jobs are affected. It also produces audit reports for migration runs.

Success: a customer can sign off a wave from the summary, and an administrator can act on every exception and
every expression rewrite from the detail sections without asking FIS for clarification.

## Operating Context

- One discovery per holding company and wave, per source environment tier (Dev/Unit, System/Test, Readiness,
  Production, DR).
- Reports are generated by FIS and delivered to the bank: opened from disk, attached to email, or printed.
- Readers check reports against BusinessObjects itself, so document IDs, folder paths and universe names must
  match what they see there.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Customer-facing HTML reports are single self-contained files: inline CSS, **no JavaScript**, no external
  fonts, scripts or images. They must work offline and when emailed, and print legibly (collapsed detail must
  have a printable equivalent).
- Discovery reports: one customer readiness report (summary, reports that will not convert, reports that will
  convert, in a single file that prints as one PDF) and an internal full inventory. Run reports: HTML, PDF
  (Windows-only, PDFsharp), CSV.
- Reason a report won't convert: structured `ReasonCode`, derived from status text until the producer emits it
  (`docs/data/DISCOVERY_JSON.md` §8.1).
- Input contract: `docs/data/discovery.schema.json` and `docs/data/DISCOVERY_JSON.md`.
- No secrets, connection strings, CUIDs or internal source IDs in customer-facing output. BusinessObjects
  document IDs are shown, because customers look them up.
- Proof of concept: data comes from sample files and in-memory stubs; there is no authentication.
- Customer-facing wording for each conversion reason is drafted in `Core/Domain/ConversionReason.cs` and listed in
  `docs/data/DISCOVERY_JSON.md` §8.2. Pending: review by whoever owns the customer relationship, because several
  actions commit FIS to act.

## Evidence on Hand

- `Data/20260925225533667-discovery.json`: realistic test bank (H548), 88 reports, 43 not converting, 62 jobs.
- `Data/20261001202032371-discovery.json`: synthetic CHAR/VARCHAR set (H999), 26 reports all ready, 12 with
  expression rewrites.
- Both are test banks; names and paths are internal. There are no customer logos, testimonials or real
  production figures, and none may be invented.

## Product Principles

1. **Say what happens to every report.** Every discovered report is accounted for as converting or not, with a
   reason. Nothing is silently dropped.
2. **Reasons before raw errors.** Lead with a plain-language reason and the action to take; keep technical
   messages available but secondary.
3. **Match what the customer sees in BusinessObjects.** Use their report names, IDs, folders and universe names
   verbatim.
4. **Works as a file.** Anything sent to a customer must stand on its own offline, in email, and on paper.
